## Doppler

Allan drew Peluva's P moving so fast that the air wraps round it. Here your three loops are set moving down along the 30° line of their openings: they bunch up in front of the head and spread open behind it, like the sound of something passing.
Motion: the head keeps sending out new loops. Each one grows, bunches in front, drifts open behind and fades.

### Peluva's process, followed

1. Start from the mark you already have: three loops, open 30° left of the top
2. Idea: set it moving along the line of its openings. Waves from a moving source bunch up in front and spread out behind
3. Three tapered strokes: thick where they bunch up, a hairline where they open
4. The rings keep going and become the pattern

### Another lettering: the mark as the o's

Small, the mark reads as an eye, so it can stand in for the two o's of loop. The second o is a mirror of the first, so they open on opposite sides. Move the pointer: the heads move the most and each ring a little less, so the whole eye turns.

### Construction

Three circles, each centred a little further behind the head as it grows, so they crowd together in front. Stroke weight follows the crowding; every opening sits on the 30° line.
Past the three strokes the same rule keeps going as hairlines: the wake, used for patterns and motion.

## Stories

Why every part is there, and how it ties back to the site: a place of many loops, each kept by one human who follows a field closely and posts what changed and what they make of it. A loop page is also that human's portfolio. Loops may look different from each other; lines are what they share.

### The opening: a number with a reason

Chosen: 30°, live on the site since 30 Sep 2026.
**Where it sits**
Every opening sits at 11 o'clock, 30° left of the top. It was there in Sahil's first sketch. On a clock that is the eleventh hour: the last hour before midnight, the moment before it is too late. It matches the thought behind the site: whatever AI turns into, until then there are humans watching.
**How wide**
The openings used to be 20° wide, a number with no reason behind it. They are now 30°: exactly one hour on a clock face, from half past ten to half past eleven. The opening is the eleventh hour itself, so the position and the width are one idea. The Doppler mark uses the same 30°.
**Does it change the look**
Very little. The drawings below put the old width, the two alternatives that were considered, and the chosen 30° side by side on a clock face.
Before The old width. No reason behind it.
22°, the halo The ring of light round the sun or moon. A loop in the sky you only see if you look up.
24°, the day One degree for every hour of the day. Someone is always watching.
30°, the eleventh hour One hour on a clock face. Placed at 11 o'clock, the opening is the eleventh hour.

### Shared by every mark

**One skeleton, three hands**
The letters of human in loop are built from circles and straight lines on one grid, the way the site is built from lines. Each mark draws them its own way: brush, an even line, three streamlines. Each loop on the site will draw its lines its own way too.
**The o's are eyes**
Loop has two o's. Give them pupils and the word keeps an eye out, which is what the humans here do: they watch their field.
**The eyes follow you**
Wherever the pointer goes, the pupils turn to it. The site looks back at its reader: you are being kept in the loop by someone who is paying attention.
**Round ends**
Every stroke ends round. Nothing on the marks is sharp; they are written by people, for people.
**Two colours a loop**
Each loop gets one ink and one paper. Any grey comes from how close the lines sit, the same rule the site already uses.
**The AI loop is black and white**
It holds both readings of where AI is going: a darker future, and an open one. Other loops pick their own pair; the ui, ux and security colours on this page are examples.

### A. Three Loops

**Drawn by the first keeper**
The mark began as Sahil's own sketch, made by the human who keeps the first loop. The brush strokes keep the marks of a hand.
**Outer loop**
The field. It has the heaviest stroke because it holds everything else.
**Middle ring**
The arms, raised and holding the loop from inside, in the same shape as the loop. The human is in sync with the field they keep.
**Inner ring**
The head: attention, the part that watches.
**S-shaped spine**
A little movement and a little joy. A person, not a pictogram.
**Legs on the loop**
The human stands on their field. In the fall the human rides the loop like a surfboard.
**One wedge through all three**
Field, hands and head open on the same ray at 11 o'clock. One way in, and every loop stays open to readers.
**Dry-brush streaks**
Where the brush ran dry, and why the outer loop fades into a light stroke at the opening. They show a hand made it; only the 16 px tab icon leaves them out.
**Draw-on**
The strokes appear in the order a hand would paint them: loop, arms, head, spine, legs.
**The fall**
The whole mark comes down the 30° line of its openings like a meteor. The pace of change is fierce; the loop takes the heat and the human inside rides it. The streamlined version shows the air lining up round the loop, the same kind of line field the site draws.
**Patterns**
Rosette: many humans round one centre. Loop field: loops waiting for their keepers, openings in step. Crowd: everyone in their own loop.
**Type**
Newsreader, a text face made for long reading on screens, for posts and views. Martian Mono for dates, labels and loop names.

### B. Peek

**A human peeking over the edge**
Someone inside the loop, looking out at what is happening. Watching the field is the whole job, so the mark is someone watching.
**The loop stays, the human changes**
The ring is the same on every loop; the one peeking out is different in each. On this page only the hair changes: bald, curls, bun, cap.
**Its ancestor**
Kilroy, the Second World War doodle of a face over a wall above the words Kilroy was here. Peek keeps the idea of presence, drops the nose and fingers, and makes the loop itself the wall.
**Stop motion and a blink**
The head ducks and comes back up in a few jerky frames, as if animated by hand, one of the directions Sahil asked for. The blink makes it alive.
**Doto**
A dot-matrix face for tickers and labels: the dot-matrix direction, and dots are what the eyes are made of.
**Patterns**
A wall of loops all watching the same thing, humans in sync. The eyes: the lettering's o's gathered together. Somebody is watching: most loops look empty until you notice one is not.

### C. Doppler

**Made the Peluva way**
Allan Peters redrew Peluva by starting from their existing mark and adding motion and meaning: a P moving so fast the air wraps round it. Doppler starts from the three loops of A and sets them moving.
**Moving along the openings**
It travels 30° off vertical, down and to the right, so the openings trail behind it at 11 o'clock, exactly where A has them.
**Crowded in front, open behind**
Waves from a moving source bunch up ahead of it and spread out behind. The human is at the front edge, where things arrive first: always on the edge.
**Thick where crowded**
Stroke weight follows the crowding and thins to a hairline where the rings open, the way grey on the site comes from line density.
**Three rings and a head**
The same three loops as A with the head at the front. C is A in motion.
**The o's**
Small, the mark reads as an eye, so it stands in for the o's of loop. The second is a mirror of the first, and every ring turns toward the pointer.
**Patterns**
Wake: the rings keep coming. Many loops: several humans moving together, their rings crossing where fields overlap. Streams: the air runs up the 30° line and bends round each human.
**Type**
Commissioner with its flair axis turned up, so stroke ends taper like the rings. Spline Sans Mono for labels.
All three are drawn in code from the same few numbers, so any of them can be adjusted, animated or recoloured per loop. Colours shown for the ui, ux and security loops are examples.
