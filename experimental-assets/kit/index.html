The idea

Arrival v2

Arrival with room to breathe. With the telescope out, a small dotted circle sways up and down as it does while he searches, then locks on the planet's dot, and the close-up opens out of it on two dashed lines and stays long enough to read. The planet is far off, so it no longer moves when he dodges; it only grows, faster and faster, while he gets smaller in the picture. The air gets a stage of its own: it feels faster (everything shakes, the streaks and the trail speed up), the trail's lines catch fire and flames lick round the front of the loop, and only then does the loop burn. On the ground he looks up at the sky he fell from, then left, then right, hears the clock tick, and walks to the edge: a slow start, a glance, then a real walk, slowing at the edge.

Arrival

Where the 404 page's meteor lands. It starts on the last frame of Fall and ends on its first. His arms come down out of the ring and he steers: rocks come at him down the line from five o'clock and he dodges them with his body and arms, the trail bending where he has been. A small planet, far off; he pulls out a telescope (click, click), sees a loop there, and points. It comes up from five o'clock, bigger and bigger; in its air the loop burns from the opening, both ends, round to his feet, until he is on one foot on the last bit and then on nothing. He drops onto the planet, flat now, gets up, dusts off, looks about, walks right to an edge, and studies a loop whose opening is going round like a clock. Then Fall begins.

Fall

The cliff, told again. The loop is there from the first frame, at the heart of the site's tunnel of lines, its opening going round like the hand of a clock. When it reaches eleven he runs and jumps, and goes in by the opening, feet first. He lands on the inside of its curve (the legs: back foot low, front foot up the curve) and the knock sends it falling down the 30 degree line with him riding it: his balance, a carve, a hop, a hand trailed along the wall. Faster, the lines outside wrap round into the site's meteor. Then the loop rocks and shakes; he reaches up, elbows bent, then arms straight toward its walls, and bends them round inside it in its own shape. The rocking stops. The mark falls on, as it does on the 404 page.

Constellation

Loops, with every loop in its place. One keeper in the AI loop casts a line; it runs to the next loop, a keeper drops in, and it runs on and branches. Keeper

What the mark means. A human at the edge of the site's tunnel of lines, watching it change, while a clock ticks. On the eleventh tick he dives in. The pressure draws his loop round him from the front, so its opening is the wake at eleven; he lands on its curve and rides it (the legs); the wind bends his arms into its shape (in sync with the field he keeps); and the field settles into rings round him. Keep Up

The field streams past and its flow turns his loop like a wheel. He walks to keep his place; it quickens, and he jogs, runs, sprints, the lines all streaks. His foot catches: he goes down on his face, the floor drags him up the side, and he tumbles over and over in one place. Then he plants his back foot, steps his front foot up the curve and comes up standing on the slope, in the stance of the mark. He wobbles, finds it, and rides the wheel instead of running it, hands on his hips. You cannot outrun the field; you can ride it.

Both Readings

The picture split down the middle, paper and ink, and his loop on the seam: both readings of where AI is going. He looks at the paper side and its lines go calm; he steps toward it and it floods the picture. A crack from the other side: its lines go wild and it floods back. Starry Night

One version, ink and paper at once: the painting's night in brush strokes. The strokes are not alike: the open sky is very fine, small strokes; round each star they grow bold the nearer they are, in rings, and thin out as they go; the great swirl and its partner are spirals, fine in the eye and bolder at the rim; a wave of middling strokes runs through the middle of the sky. A crescent in an orb of fine rings. Paper mountains in ink strokes, a dark village with paper houses and a church spire, a black cypress of three flames, and a small figure in light, watching. The swirls turn, the stars breathe, the sky shimmers, the cypress sways.

Starry Night II

A copy of Starry Night, sky only: no village, the figure on the mountains, more sky so the moon is bigger. The wind is one stream of lines: it rises from behind the cypress, wraps over the great whirlpool and its inner lines roll up into it like a carpet; the rest swing down in an S, roll into the second whirlpool, and flow out under the moon. Every line of a whirlpool is a line of the stream. The open sky is fine strokes traced like the fingerprint's ridges, lying along the stream beside it. The stars are lights it flows round: a core and rings of strokes, bold close in and thinning out. A crescent moon with its dark side lit in fine rings. The stars breathe, the moon glows, the sky shimmers, the cypress sways.

Starry Night III

Starry Night II's whirlpools with the stream running through them. The stream comes in from behind the cypress and parts at the great whirlpool: the upper half arches over the top and rolls into the spiral; the lower half bends down and passes underneath.
